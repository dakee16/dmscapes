## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Strongly authored for Dormscape; a generic template could not swap in unchanged (top-decile specificity). The hero is the product, not a picture of it: RoomPlanner is a live miniature where tapping a vibe recolors the floor plan, swaps the shoppable Amazon list, and slides the budget bar under a real cap with an "Everything fits" badge. Domain data is everywhere (real halls, real dimensions, "even which way the door swings"). The graph-paper motif is semantic (grid = floor plan = measured to the inch), and the oversized bleeding footer wordmark is a genuine brand signature. The only drift toward category-generic is the Schools card grid and the FAQ accordion pattern, both of which still carry domain-specific content.

**Deterministic scan:** detect.mjs over the 9 homepage markup files returned **0 findings, exit 0 (clean)**, both combined and per-file. Validated genuine: a synthetic canary with `border-l-4`, `bg-clip-text bg-gradient-to-r`, and `text-gray-400 bg-blue-500` correctly fired 3 findings, so the empty result is a real pass. Notably the grid-background rule did not fire on the intentional graph-paper texture, and palette/font/motion rules did not fire on the cobalt/amber/ink + framer-motion brand elements. This corroborates the LLM specificity verdict: no mechanical generic-AI patterns present.

**Visual overlays:** No user-visible overlay available. The site's CSP `script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline' 'unsafe-eval' https://us-assets.i.posthog.com` blocked the external detect.js overlay (verbatim CSP violation captured). Screenshots were used as evidence instead. Both agents independently observed blank mid-page regions in full-page captures; this is a framer-motion `whileInView` artifact (sections arm to opacity 0 until scrolled into view), not missing content, confirmed by reduced-motion captures rendering fully.

## Overall Impression

A genuinely well-authored, product-specific landing page whose interactive hero and relentless domain detail put it in the top tier for its category. The gap between "very good" and "impeccable" is small and cheap to close: one real layout bug on the primary device, a credibility wobble from internal number contradictions, and the total absence of any third-party social proof for the paying parent. Biggest single opportunity: fix the mobile nav overflow, because mobile is the primary context and it is the literal first impression.

## What's Working

1. **The hero preview demonstrates the differentiator instead of claiming it.** Proof over promise: a skeptic sees a real room fit real furniture under a real budget in one glance and can poke it. Biggest persuasion asset and the emotional peak, and it is the actual product logic so it cannot ring false.
2. **Relentless domain specificity is the trust mechanism.** Real hall names, real dimensions, door-swing detail, and bespoke per-vibe illustrations make the "we have your real room" claim feel true at a glance.
3. **Reassurance is choreographed at every commit point.** Each CTA is immediately followed by the exact objection-killer ("No account needed. Most rooms take under a minute."), and the upsell uses zero scarcity. Removes friction precisely where a student-plus-parent hesitates.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Mobile nav overflows and clips the "Log in" button** — `components/Nav.tsx`
- Why it matters: At 390px the header row measures ~414px, so the page scrolls horizontally ~24px and "Log in" is cut off. Mobile is the primary context (first-years, one-handed, over the summer). A clipped control plus sideways jiggle is the first impression and reads as broken, undercutting the trustworthy/real-data signal before the value prop lands.
- Fix: Below `lg`, drop the standalone header "Plan my room" button (the hero CTA is one screen away) or move "Log in" into the overflow/ProfileMenu; shrink the wordmark if needed. Guarantee total width <= viewport, no horizontal scroll.
- Suggested command: /impeccable adapt (with /impeccable harden for responsive robustness)

**[P1] Contradictory school count undercuts the core claim** — `components/HowItWorks.tsx`
- Why it matters: How It Works says "official housing docs for 50+ schools," while the hero ("16 schools preloaded"), the FAQ ("we're at 16 schools"), and the 12-card grid all say/imply 16. The product's entire credibility is "we have real data for your room." A visible internal contradiction about the count is exactly what a skeptical parent or stress-tester seizes on.
- Fix: Reconcile to the true number (16) everywhere, or reframe How It Works to "pulled from official housing docs, school by school" with no number. Note: "Five styles, from Minimalist to Preppy" in the same component also contradicts the nine vibes shown below.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P2] Soft close: no final CTA before the footer** — `app/page.tsx` (FAQ section)
- Why it matters: The page ends on the FAQ accordion + a small "Still curious? Read the full FAQ." The last strong ask is the mid-page dark block. By peak-end logic, the highest-intent visitors (who read to the bottom) hit a low-energy ending with no primary "Plan my room" to convert that intent.
- Fix: Add a final "Plan my room for free" closing CTA after the FAQ, echoing the hero ask + reassurance line, so the end mirrors the beginning.
- Suggested command: /impeccable shape (or /impeccable overdrive for a bolder close)

**[P2] "Five styles" vs nine shown** — `components/HowItWorks.tsx`
- Why it matters: Step 2 reads "Five styles, from Minimalist to Preppy," but the showcase directly below shows nine cards (five free + four Plus-badged). A skimmer reads five, counts nine: a small credibility ding and a missed chance to make the Plus upsell legible ("5 free, 9 with Plus").
- Fix: "Five free styles (nine with Plus)" or drop the count.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P3] Hero preview offers Plus-gated vibes with no marker** — `components/RoomPlanner.tsx` vs `components/Vibes.tsx`
- Why it matters: The hero switcher lets you freely pick "Gamer"/"Team Spirit," which the Vibes showcase flags as Plus-only. A visitor who falls for the hero "Gamer" preview later learns it is paywalled. Mildly bait-y; a stress-tester notices.
- Fix: Show a free-only set in the hero, or add a subtle "Plus" marker on the gated chips.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify / /impeccable polish

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer):** Mostly well served (one obvious blue CTA, plain three-step framing). Flags: "50+ schools" then a short list may make Jordan wonder "is mine even here?" before spotting "Add my school" far down. Tappable hero chips could make Jordan think the hero *is* the planner.

**Casey (distracted, one-handed mobile):** The P1 clipped-nav + horizontal scroll is the opening experience. Also, on mobile the Vibes become a horizontal snap-carousel showing ~1.5 cards, so Casey likely never realizes there are nine vibes (or that four are Plus) — the range and upsell desktop users see at a glance are hidden. Good: primary CTA is above the fold at 390x844.

**Riley (stress tester):** Latches onto (a) "50+" vs "16," (b) "Five styles" vs nine, (c) hero letting you pick Plus-only vibes with no gate, (d) "Room in 3D" (Soon) routing to /pricing, (e) footer socials pointing to bare tiktok.com / instagram.com with no handle (reads as placeholder). Each small; together they chip at "real and finished."

**Paying parent (project persona, skimming for trust + cost):** Cost clarity is a strength (Free, no account, $7.99/$19.99 one-time, honest affiliate explanation). Trust red flags: the school-count contradiction erodes exactly the "real data" claim the parent is verifying; placeholder-looking socials weaken legitimacy; and there is zero third-party social proof anywhere (no testimonials, no "rooms planned," no press or school logos). A parent seeking reassurance before spending has nothing external to lean on.

## Minor Observations

- No em/en dashes anywhere in homepage copy (grep-verified across all nine source files); ranges correctly use hyphens ("$200-$1,500"). Brand rule holds.
- Footer social links go to bare tiktok.com / instagram.com (no handles) — reads as placeholder; real handles would double as social proof.
- Hero and How It Works use different illustrative budgets ($438/$500 vs $612/$650) — fine as examples, not a defect.
- Desktop nav carries 7 interactive targets; borderline busy but standard.
- The bleeding footer wordmark holds up on mobile as well as desktop.
- The black circle bottom-left in captures is the Next.js dev indicator, not UI.

## Questions to Consider

1. What if the hero preview *were* the funnel entrance ("Want this for your school?") instead of a separate "Plan my room" click?
2. What if the visitor picked their school in the hero and instantly saw their real room, collapsing the value prop into one undeniable action?
3. What if there were any third-party trust signal (a rooms-planned counter, two student quotes, an "as seen on TikTok" strip) for the skeptical paying parent, who currently gets none?
4. What if mobile vibes were a 2-up grid or carried a visible "9 vibes ->" affordance, so mobile users grasp the range and the Plus upsell that desktop users get for free?
